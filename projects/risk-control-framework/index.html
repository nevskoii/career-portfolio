Enterprise Risk & Internal Control

A practical case study of how I approached business process risks, internal controls and operational assurance in a data centre environment.

Enterprise risk frameworkBusiness process reviewsRCSA & control assessmentOperational resilience

01 / The business challenge

Data centre operations combine financial, commercial and technology processes. Risks can arise when approval rules, operational records, contracts and actual service delivery do not align. The challenge was to maintain a consistent view of material risks and the controls designed to prevent, detect and address them, while making findings actionable for process owners.

My work covered the full control lifecycle: identifying process risks, assessing control design and operating effectiveness, discussing exceptions with stakeholders and following up on corrective actions.

02 / My role and approach

As Lead Risk and Internal Control Specialist at Rostelecom (Data Centres), I contributed to an enterprise-wide framework covering approximately 90 risks and 250 controls and delivered 10+ end-to-end risk-based internal business process reviews.

  • Map the process: identify objectives, key activities, owners, systems, approvals and relevant evidence.
  • Assess the risks: consider operational and financial consequences, likelihood, existing mitigations and residual exposure.
  • Review the controls: distinguish preventive, detective and corrective measures; assess ownership, frequency, evidence and segregation of duties.
  • Test and discuss: perform walkthroughs, review selected transactions and records, discuss exceptions with process owners and document findings.
  • Follow through: agree Corrective Action Plans, monitor remediation and report material themes to management.

04 / Selected findings and outcomes

Revenue assurance

Identified colocation discounts exceeding approved limits by up to 30% and unauthorised long-term free rack reservations. Findings prompted reviews of customer contracts, pricing and revenue controls.

Contract cost analysis

Built an Excel model comparing contracted and actual power consumption across approximately 4,000 racks. Flagged electricity charges up to 4.5% above contractual terms and prompted reviews of 42 counterparty agreements.

07 / What this work taught me

Controls become useful when they are specific enough to execute, supported by evidence and clearly owned. Risk registers alone do not improve a process: the value comes from connecting risks to operational decisions, identifying exceptions and making remediation measurable.

My approach is to link business objectives, control evidence and corrective action — with enough analytical detail to support a decision and enough clarity for the process owner to act.
